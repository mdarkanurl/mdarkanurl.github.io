Answers of Question 2

Answer

When John joins the queue, the doctor is already seeing Peter. The estimated waiting time for John depends on how much time remains in Peter's consultation, plus the full consultation time of the 4 patients still waiting ahead of John (excluding Peter, who is currently being seen).

Since the average consultation time is 3 minutes, the remaining time for Peter equals 3 minutes minus the time Peter has already spent in the consultation room. The 4 remaining patients each take 3 minutes.

(a) Peter has been in the consultation room for 2 minutes

Remaining time for Peter's consultation = 3 − 2 = 1 minute.

Time for the 4 remaining patients ahead of John = 4 × 3 = 12 minutes.

Estimated waiting time for John = Remaining time for Peter + Time for 4 patients = 1 + 12 = 13 minutes
(b) Peter has been in the consultation room for 5 minutes

Peter has already exceeded the average consultation time of 3 minutes. In this system, we treat the consultation as finishing at any moment — since the average is 3 minutes, we can assume Peter's session is effectively overdue and may end imminently. For estimation purposes, the remaining time for Peter is taken as 0 minutes (or negligibly small).

Time for the 4 remaining patients ahead of John = 4 × 3 = 12 minutes.

Estimated waiting time for John = Remaining time for Peter + Time for 4 patients = 0 + 12 = 12 minutes

Note: This estimate is not strictly accurate. The value of 12 minutes assumes that each remaining patient will take exactly the average consultation time. However, in reality, if any of the remaining patients take less than the average time (or if delays vary), the total waiting time can decrease. Similarly, if consultations exceed the average, the waiting time will increase.

Case Study 2

Answers of Question 1

Answer

Since the clinic has 2 doctors working simultaneously, the queue is processed faster compared to a single-doctor clinic. Patients are dynamically assigned to whichever doctor becomes available first.

Doctor A takes an average of 3 minutes per patient, while Doctor B takes an average of 4 minutes per patient.

To estimate John's waiting time, we calculate the combined processing speed of both doctors.

Doctor A consultation time = 3 minutes per patient Doctor B consultation time = 4 minutes per patient In 12 minutes: Doctor A can see: 12 ÷ 3 = 4 patients Doctor B can see: 12 ÷ 4 = 3 patients Total patients processed: 4 + 3 = 7 patients Average processing time per patient: 12 ÷ 7 ≈ 1.71 minutes John has 10 patients ahead of him Estimated waiting time: 10 × 1.71 ≈ 17.1 minutes Estimated waiting time for John ≈ 17 minutes

Therefore, John's estimated waiting time is approximately 17 minutes.

Note: This is an estimated average waiting time. Actual waiting time may vary depending on how long individual consultations take, emergencies, or unexpected delays.

Answers of Question 2

Answer

At the time John joins the queue, there are 10 patients ahead of him. Doctor A is immediately available, while Doctor B is currently seeing Lucas.

Doctor A takes an average of 3 minutes per patient, while Doctor B takes an average of 4 minutes per patient.

Since Lucas has already spent 2 minutes with Doctor B, the estimated remaining consultation time for Lucas is:

Remaining time for Lucas = 4 − 2 = 2 minutes

This means:

• Doctor A starts processing patients immediately.
• Doctor B joins the queue processing after 2 minutes.

To estimate John's waiting time, we calculate how many patients both doctors can complete before John becomes next in line.

Doctor A processes 1 patient every 3 minutes In 12 minutes, Doctor A can process: 12 ÷ 3 = 4 patients Doctor B becomes available after 2 minutes Remaining active time within 12 minutes: 12 − 2 = 10 minutes Doctor B processes 1 patient every 4 minutes Patients Doctor B can process in 10 minutes: 10 ÷ 4 = 2.5 patients Approximately: 2 patients fully completed Total patients completed in about 12 minutes: 4 + 2 = 6 patients

After approximately 12 minutes, around 6 patients ahead of John will have been completed. There will still be about 4 patients ahead of him.

Remaining patients ahead of John = 10 − 6 = 4 patients

Now both doctors are actively processing the queue together.

Combined processing rate: Doctor A: 1 patient every 3 minutes = 1/3 patient per minute Doctor B: 1 patient every 4 minutes = 1/4 patient per minute Combined rate: 1/3 + 1/4 = 7/12 patients per minute Time required for remaining 4 patients: 4 ÷ (7/12) = 48/7 ≈ 6.86 minutes

Therefore, John's total estimated waiting time is:

Total waiting time ≈ 12 + 6.86 ≈ 18.86 minutes Estimated waiting time ≈ 19 minutes

Note: This is an estimated waiting time based on average consultation durations. Actual waiting time may vary depending on emergencies, consultation complexity, or unexpected delays.
